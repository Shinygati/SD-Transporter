SD TRANSPORTER - POKEWALKER COMPANION USER GUIDE
Version 1.6.2 - Updated: 6 November 2026

This guide covers the 3DS, NDS and Android companions and the iPhone source
port. The phone apps use the same walker progress and activities. The iPhone
port currently requires a separate Apple SDK build; no iPhone binary is included.

1. INSTALL OR UPDATE

The release ZIP has one folder per setup: DS or DS Lite - flashcart,
DSi - flashcart, 3DS - flashcart, DSi - custom firmware and 3DS - custom
firmware. Open START HERE.txt, pick the one folder that matches your console
and follow its README.txt. Each folder already includes TWiLight Menu++ 27.25.1
with the SD Transporter changes applied, so nothing else needs downloading.

Copy the CONTENTS of each "Copy to ..." folder to the root of the card it
names, merging folders. Keep your existing game saves and walker files; they
are not overwritten. Back up the card first.

3DS:
- Run 3ds/pokewalker3ds/pokewalker3ds.3dsx from the Homebrew Launcher.
  A CIA for the companion is not included in 1.6.2.
NDS / DS Lite / DSi (and 3DS in DS mode):
- Launch roms/nds/Pokewalker-NDS.nds from TWiLight Menu++, with working
  SD/DLDI access.
Android:
- Install the signed 1.6.2 APK over the existing app. Keeping the installed
  app's data preserves the active walker, saved consoles and handoff records.
  Never uninstall the app while it holds your Pokemon.

Update the console and phone companions together when updating sync features.
See README.md for the GBA migration, Poke Transfer and other features.

2. CREATE AND OPEN A WALKER

In TWiLight Menu++, highlight HeartGold or SoulSilver and press Y. Set
Bootstrap: to Nightly, then highlight "Virtual Pokewalker: Off" and press A so
it reads "Virtual Pokewalker: On". Launch the game as normal.
Use HGSS's normal Pokewalker menu to register the walker and send a Pokemon
on a stroll. The virtual connection is triggered by pressing SELECT on the
normal Pokewalker connection/search screen. SELECT is the walker's button,
like the button on a real Pokewalker: press it once, don't hold it. Each
connection needs its own press; wait a second or two after a transfer
before connecting again.

SELECT + LEFT RESETS THE WALKER. Holding SELECT and LEFT together for about two
seconds while HGSS is searching wipes the virtual walker back to a brand-new,
unregistered one, the same as deleting the .walker file: trainer registration,
partner, caught Pokemon, items, watts and step history are all cleared. A quick
or accidental press of both does nothing. It only works on the search screen,
before the connection starts, never during normal play or a transfer. Use it only
when you deliberately want a fresh walker (for example, one registered to a
different save that HGSS refuses). If you press it by accident, stop, and
restore the newest "prelaunch" snapshot from before the wipe using Recovery
(section 8). A prelaunch snapshot is saved automatically before every launch.
If a Pokemon was out on a stroll when you wiped it, just restart the game: the
automatic repair (section 7) puts the walker back.

Real HGSS cartridges (DSi/3DS in DSi mode with console SD access):
Launch roms/nds/Virtual-Pokewalker-Cartridge.nds, insert the cartridge when
asked, press A to check it, then A to start. Its walker is saved on the
console SD as roms/nds/saves/Cartridge-<game code>-<header CRC>.walker and
opens in the companions like any other walker. Keep the SD card inserted.

Close HGSS before opening its walker in a companion. The game creates a 64 KiB
.walker file next to its save, for example:
  /roms/nds/saves/Pokemon HeartGold.walker

The console companion searches only /roms and its subfolders. Select the
walker using the D-pad or touch screen and press A to open it. The preview
shows its trainer name and public five-digit Trainer ID when available.
A registered empty walker earns steps and watts but no Pokemon joins it; send
a partner from HGSS. A blank/unregistered file is not ready.

Android and iPhone receive a walker from a console over Wi-Fi; ordinary
recovery exports do not automatically become an active phone walker.

3. WALKING SETTINGS

Open Settings to choose your step source and time-based rate.

3DS:
- Physical 3DS pedometer counting is available separately from the time counter.
- Time-based counting is off by default. Physical counting can remain enabled
  while time-based counting is off.
- Time-based progress counts while the app is open, including open-app sleep.
NDS:
- DS hardware has no built-in pedometer. It uses time-based counting, enabled
  by default, while the companion is running, including open-app sleep.
Android / iPhone:
- Physical phone steps are the default; permit motion/physical activity access
  when asked. Time-based counting is off by default.
- Select the desired step source in Settings. Pausing stops credited progress.
- Android uses its ongoing walking notification for background tracking.
- iPhone may recover physical step history supplied by iOS when reopening;
  its simulated timer runs only while the app is active.

The default simulated rate is one step every 10 seconds. Closed-app time does
not generate simulated steps. Normally, 20 credited steps earn one watt.
Stroll steps and lifetime steps are different counters. An ordinary timer
change keeps progress already earned. The trainer card shows additional totals.

4. ACTIVITIES AND YOUR PACK

Poke Radar costs 10 watts. Follow the rustling grass patches, then choose attack,
evade or a Poke Ball in battle. A successful catch names the Pokemon caught.
Dowsing costs 3 watts. Search the grass patches using the near/far hints.
Your pack holds up to three caught Pokemon and three items. If it is full,
choose a slot to replace or leave the new reward.

Catches and item finds are saved immediately and get recovery snapshots.
If saving fails, keep the app open and retry saving. Do not treat an error as
a completed save. Android offers Retry saving; consoles show a save-error
screen. Phone recovery copies remain accessible in Settings.

Before every save, all companions check the walker. A damaged copy of a
mirrored record is repaired from its good copy automatically. If both copies
are bad, or a Pokemon in the walker is malformed (species, level, item, form
or moves), the save is refused ("Walker data check") so HGSS never receives
broken data. Nothing on the card is changed; keep the app open and use
Recovery. Consoles apply the same check to a walker returned from a phone.

Pokemon received in HGSS from the walker now get 0 PP in empty move slots.
HGSS used to leave the PP of the default move that slot had before; the
1.6.1 bootstrap corrects this while the walker is connected.

Normal course encounters are shiny-locked. Walking events may find rewards.
A registered empty walker earns steps and watts, but from 1.6.1 no Pokemon
joins it: HGSS builds a joined partner as an event Pokemon from walker data
the companions never had, so those partners came back illegal. A partner that
joined under an older version is still on its walker. These companions
implement supported original-style activities; they do not execute original
Pokewalker firmware. Sections 14-17 describe original-style controls, optional
rewards, visits, Bluetooth battles and supported special-course activation.

5. CONNECT CONSOLE AND PHONE

Connect both devices to the same local Wi-Fi network. On the console, open
Settings > Phone sync. Keep that screen open and note its IP address.
On Android, scroll down on the main screen past Settings to Console Wi-Fi sync.
On iPhone, open its console sync screen. Enter the console IP address.
No six-digit code is required. The default port is 30820. Android accepts an
optional IP:port; on iPhone enter the IPv4 address only.
Android's Save this console and Saved consoles buttons store a named address.
If the console's IP changes later, update or save its new address.

To send CONSOLE -> PHONE:
1. Open the intended walker on the console and enter Phone sync.
2. On the phone, choose Receive Pokemon from console.
3. Wait for the phone's received confirmation.
4. The selected phone profile now owns the active Pokemon. The console copy is
   locked. Other active phone profiles continue earning steps.

To send PHONE -> CONSOLE:
1. On the same console, reopen the matching walker's Phone sync screen.
2. On the phone, choose Return Pokemon.
3. Wait for the confirmed return. The console is unlocked and the phone's
   active copy is removed from that profile. Other profiles are unchanged.
   Recovery snapshots are kept separately.

Do not change Wi-Fi configuration during a handoff. A locked or pending copy
cannot be used for activities. If a return is unconfirmed, retry with the same
console and session rather than starting another handoff.

The current LAN protocol is unencrypted HTTP. Use a local network you trust.
Guest networks with client isolation may stop the devices reaching each other.
No developer server receives the walker.

6. DS LITE WI-FI SETUP

Configure Wi-Fi BEFORE checking the walker out to a phone.
Use Settings > Wi-Fi setup, or Y on the NDS walker list when available.
DS Lite supports compatible open or WEP networks, not modern WPA-only networks.
For WEP, enter the supported hexadecimal key (10, 26 or 32 digits).
Choose a blank-name result to enter a hidden SSID, or press Y inside Wi-Fi setup
to enter the network name and run a directed search. SSIDs are case-sensitive.
The Wi-Fi profile contains your WEP key and is stored on the card; keep it private.
After setup, leave the scan screen and open Phone sync before using the phone.
On 3DS/DSi, configure the network using the platform's supported settings.

7. RETURN PROGRESS TO HGSS

Return the Pokemon from the phone to its original console first, if necessary.
Save and close the companion before launching HGSS. Use the game's normal
Pokewalker menu to return from the stroll or receive supported gifts/rewards.
Keep each walker paired with the HGSS save that created its registration.
Recovery does not merge separate Pokemon, trainers or newly reset strolls.

The walker saves its changes to the card as soon as the connection ends, while
HGSS is saving the game. Let HGSS finish saving before you reset or turn the
console off.

AUTOMATIC REPAIR. Every launch first compares the walker with the HGSS save
(same trainer only, never a walker that is out on a phone):
- Save says nothing is out but the walker holds a Pokemon (for example a send
  that reached the walker but the game did not finish, which shows "a Pokemon
  is already on the Pokewalker"): the leftover copy is removed. The game
  still has that Pokemon in its party or PC.
- Save says a Pokemon is out but the walker is empty or wiped: the walker is
  put back from <walker>.walker.lastgood, or the newest matching prelaunch
  snapshot.
- Save says nothing is out but the walker has lost its registration (HGSS
  says "This Pokewalker has not been registered"), usually because a pairing
  was cut off part way: the last walker registered to the same trainer is put
  back, with its steps and watts. A walker you wiped on purpose with
  SELECT + LEFT, or a .walker file you deleted, is left as a new walker.
The .lastgood file is kept next to the walker whenever walker and save agree;
only the launcher uses it. Any repair is described in
_nds/nds-bootstrap/pokewalker-repair.txt, and the walker as it was before the
repair stays in transfers/backups. The real-cartridge launcher cannot read the
cartridge save first, so it does not repair automatically.

8. BACKUPS AND DELETION

Recovery history keeps at most 10 snapshots PER WALKER. A newly written
snapshot is verified before the oldest history entries are removed.
Console histories are grouped by original walker path. Phone histories are
grouped by the stable trainer/device identity, not by a changing sync token.
Older existing histories are trimmed when that walker next creates a snapshot.
Active files, pending handoff safety copies, locks and ownership receipts are
outside this history limit. They protect unfinished transfers.

Backups never load automatically as active Pokemon. Open Recovery deliberately.
On console, histories are under the walker's adjacent transfers/backups folder.

Console controls:
- Settings: X opens Recovery copies / delete.
- Walker file list: SELECT opens histories, including missing active files.
- Locked-walker screen: R opens Backups.
- Recovery browser: Y changes walker; L/R changes snapshot; B goes back.
- START opens Restore selected backup; inspect it and press A to confirm.
  The current file is archived first, and any outstanding phone lease is
  revoked. Restoring an old checkpoint can duplicate Pokemon already received
  in HGSS; use it only when recovery is needed. Trainer mismatches are refused.
- SELECT opens missing-file recovery for adjacent .tmp/.bak candidates.
- A deletes the selected snapshot AFTER confirmation.
- X deletes the selected walker's active file AFTER confirmation. A verified
  recovery snapshot is preserved first. A phone handoff is revoked if needed.

Phone controls:
- Settings > Recovery copies lets you export or delete individual snapshots.
- Delete active walker removes the local active Pokemon after preserving a
  recovery snapshot. It does not automatically unlock an offline console.
- Ownership/session records are separate and cannot be deleted in this browser.

9. INTERRUPTED TRANSFERS

If receiving on the phone fails, the console keeps a locked recovery snapshot.
Retry Receive on the same console. If a return fails, the phone retains a frozen
pending copy until it gets confirmation that the console saved the data.

If the phone cannot be recovered, use the console's Force unlock / Recover
option and confirm it deliberately. This restores the console's earlier
checkout snapshot and permanently revokes that phone session. Progress earned
only on the lost phone is not included. That old phone session cannot later
return over the recovered walker; a fresh handoff is required.

A force recovery is not a progress merge. Do not keep using an old exported
copy as another active Pokemon.

10. MISSING .WALKER AFTER AN INTERRUPTED SAVE

If you find .walker.tmp or .walker.bak but the final .walker is missing,
keep those files. The .tmp may contain newer rewards than the .bak.

On the console:
1. Rescan /roms if needed, then press SELECT for Recovery.
2. Press Y to select the missing walker's history.
3. Press SELECT inside the recovery browser for Recover missing file.
   START also opens this flow when there are no history snapshots.
4. Use L/R to inspect available verified candidates and trainer/pack details.
5. Press A to deliberately restore the chosen candidate, or B to cancel.
6. Open the recovered file from the normal list.

Recovery can use .tmp, .replace-old, .bak and .bak.tmp candidates. It keeps the
source files, refuses to overwrite an existing active walker and does not bypass
a phone lock. A phone-owned walker must use its normal revoking recovery flow.

11. BACKUP DATES

The 3DS companion reads actual SD modification dates through its native
filesystem API for display and oldest-first retention. If no valid file date
can be read, it shows Unknown date rather than 1970 or an invented date.
The displayed date follows the recorded file time and the console's clock;
changing the system clock can affect the dates of later snapshots.

For source, compilation, limitations and project credits, see README.md,
BUILDING-WINDOWS.md and CREDITS.md.

12. MULTIPLE WALKERS ON ONE PHONE

Open Walker profiles from the phone home screen. The existing single walker
becomes Walker 1 without moving or deleting its journal or recovery copies.
Use Add walker profile to create a separate slot, then receive that walker's
Pokemon from its console. Rename profiles to something recognizable, such as
HeartGold and SoulSilver. Select a profile to view it, play activities, change
its settings, return it, or manage its recovery copies.

ALL active profiles earn steps. Each keeps a separate sensor baseline, timer,
progress, pack, console address/session and recovery folder. Paused, revoked,
return-pending and currently syncing profiles do not earn steps. Changing the
viewed profile does not pause others. Walking Settings affect the selected
profile; Android's notification Pause all stops all profiles.

A profile binds to the received trainer NAME and public Trainer ID. Later
receives must match both. The console refuses a mismatch before checking out
the walker; the phone also validates received data. Profiles retain their
binding after a normal return. Deleting the active Pokemon clears the binding;
the empty profile can then receive from another trainer. Saves with identical trainer names and IDs
are treated as the same trainer; these checks do not distinguish those saves.
Existing per-session checks prevent returning through an unrelated handoff.
Profile changes are blocked during a transfer.

Update console and phone companions together for the pre-checkout trainer
check. A previously empty legacy phone profile binds on its next receive.
Recovery snapshots remain recovery-only and never become playable profiles.

13. BATTLE PRESENTATION AND SAVE SAFETY

Phone battles use the original two-frame partner/opponent images stored in the
walker, rather than enlarging the home portrait for battle. Attack, evade and
Poke Ball sequences display motion, HP changes and catch confirmation. Their
presentation timing is recreated; original retail firmware is not executed.
Catches with space in the pack are saved BEFORE the animation is played, so
leaving the screen cannot discard an already stored catch. A full pack still
requires a replacement choice. Console battle sprite frames are preserved.

The NDS companion disables filesystem write-back caching for its storage mount
so verification reaches DLDI media. Both console companions recheck the final
.walker after return lock cleanup. A receipt alone never confirms a missing,
empty or invalid final walker. An unconfirmed phone return stays locked for
retry/recovery. Keep its app data while retrying.

A partial watt can finish in fewer than 20 additional steps. Subsequent full
watts still require 20 steps; walking events may grant separate bonus watts.

14. ORIGINAL-STYLE CONTROLS AND OPTIONAL REWARDS

Tap the Pokewalker on the Android/iPhone home screen to open its original-style
LCD and three buttons. Left/right select; the centre button confirms. Progress,
catches, items, sound and contrast settings belong to the selected profile.
The original uploaded graphics and sound scores are read from your own data.
Retail firmware is not executed; audio and animation timing are recreated.

Expanded items are OFF by default. Opt in under Settings on each phone profile,
or press SELECT in console Settings for reward options (A enables, X disables).
Dowsing uses the expanded rates in section 18. Normal walking events are unchanged.

15. VISITS AND FRIENDLY BATTLES

Choose Connect another Pokewalker, then Another profile or Nearby phone.
Same-phone profiles can visit once per pair each day. Both gifts are saved
together. Nearby visits and battles use Bluetooth LE, separate from console
Wi-Fi transfers. Keep both apps open; choose the same activity on both phones,
host on one and search/connect on the other. Grant Bluetooth permissions when
asked. Older Android versions also require location permission for discovery;
the app does not read your geographic location.

Both players confirm a friendly battle before paying 30 watts each. Use the
three controls to select and confirm retained moves. PP, power, accuracy and
priority are used; status moves brace against the next attack. Full game stats,
type matchups and other special effects are not simulated. Battle progress is
separate from the Pokemon's HGSS stats. Only the winner receives an item; claim
or leave a pending reward from Home before starting another battle or returning.
Expanded battle rewards require opt-in on BOTH phones. Otherwise, all rewards
come from the current course, with its rarer entries weighted more heavily.
If settings differ, both apps warn before acceptance. See section 18 for rates.

An interrupted paid battle can be resumed against the same partner without
another entry charge; it starts a fresh battle board. Saved completed results
are restored on retry. Visits and battles never move or replace either partner.

16. RETURNING WHEN CONSOLE FILES ARE MISSING

The console file list remains usable with no walker files. Press Y, select the
matching HGSS ROM under /roms, press A, then confirm Receive phone copy with A.
On the phone, select Return Pokemon. If a revoked-session recovery prompt is
shown, follow it only with this console recovery screen open. The final .walker
must exist and verify before success. An existing active Pokemon or another
phone's lease is refused rather than replaced.

On the phone, deleting an active Pokemon preserves a recovery copy and clears
that profile's trainer pairing. A new receive can bind the empty profile to a
different trainer. Returning normally keeps the original pairing.

17. SPECIAL AND EVENT COURSES

From the console file list press Y, choose the HGSS game, press X for Courses,
then A to confirm. A matching raw 512 KiB HGSS save is required. The app validates
its identity and checksums and preserves the original in saves/transfers/backups
before enabling supported special-course flags. Select a course from HGSS when
sending a Pokemon. Language-exclusive unsupported entries are left disabled;
ordinary watt-based course progression remains unchanged.

Save checks cover missing-file returns, final-file verification, interrupted
profile writes, malformed Bluetooth packets, storage alignment and, from 1.6.1,
walker data validation before every save and on phone returns. Physical
radio/console compatibility still depends on the device. Keep independent save
backups and update the phone and console companions together.

19. PHONE DISPLAY

The device shell uses the exact image supplied by the project owner. Its
sample Pikachu and counters are covered at runtime by the live LCD. Browsing
keeps the current course scene, Pokemon, steps and pack indicators visible.
Course, watts and named pack contents remain in the profile details. During
battles only the LCD changes; the shell and buttons stay in place. Tap the
device to enable Classic mode. Only the Pokewalker and the Classic mode
toggle are shown. Tap the three buttons on the device image to navigate
its menus. Hold the centre image button to return to the main LCD while browsing.
Settings > Menu button hold offers 0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 or 3 seconds (default: 1 second).
This setting is saved on the phone and applies to every profile. Left and right wrap through menu options.
Turn Classic mode off to show the navigation controls and profile details;
choose Back to app to return to the full app controls.
